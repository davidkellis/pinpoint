    Each event gets its own candidate map. A match from one event is unverified.

    Automatic search uses only strong acoustic candidates by default, including for walks. Strong means additional signal checks passed, not a confirmed aircraft or a confidence percentage. Weak-fragment diagnostics are available only for independent screening.

    Consensus search offers every strong curve below 2.5 kHz and looks for locations where at least two of them are explained by different aircraft under one recording clock; curves no aircraft explains are left out. Each candidate area is then checked against the recording's loudness: aircraft predicted to pass close by should be audible. Results list every separated area that fits almost as well, so ambiguity stays visible.

    Automatic joint matching builds consistent groups of two or more strong curves. It fits one location and recording clock on training intervals, checks reserved audio, and repeats the complete search at eight incorrect recording times. This can be expensive across a whole state. Unmatched tones are not assumed to be planes or silence. Turn off automatic selection to require all your reviewed flyovers to match together. Surviving candidates receive a denser grid search.

    Models use 3D flight paths, terrain elevation, acoustic travel time, and radial Doppler shift. Jets are included unless you explicitly exclude them. Silence constraints require reviewed intervals and an explicit audibility radius. Motion within an event and wind are not fitted; scores are not probabilities.
